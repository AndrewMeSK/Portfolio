The HR Job Evaluator Agent

An AI agent that reads a job posting and evaluates it against role, market and quality criteria, giving HR leaders and consultants a structured, consistent read in seconds instead of a manual pass. It flags gaps, suggests fixes, and hands back a clear recommendation, so the human in the loop spends their time deciding, not screening.

  • Parses raw job postings into structured, comparable fields
  • Scores each posting against role and market benchmarks
  • Flags missing or conflicting requirements before they go live
  • Uses few-shot examples and a strict output schema to keep evaluations consistent
  • Outputs a recommendation the HR consultant can act on immediately

// How I write a PRD

How I Turn Chaos Into PRDs

PRD templates are everywhere: what actually matters is the order you go through them in, and knowing why each section earns its spot. This is the version that's worked best for me across SaaS, EdTech and HR products.

01

Overview / Objective

What are we building, and why? I keep it to two or three lines. If I can't explain the objective that simply, the scope isn't ready yet.

02

Problem Statement / Background

The user pain or business need driving the initiative, in plain language. No jargon: just what's broken, for who, and why it matters right now.

03

Goals & Success Metrics

What we're actually trying to achieve, tied to numbers I can check later. If a goal can't be measured, it gets rewritten until it can.

04

User Stories

Written clearly, as a [user], I want [action], so that [outcome], with the acceptance criteria included right alongside each one: different roles, permissions, or specific steps that have to be met before it's considered done.

05

Wireframes

Screenshots and links to Figma, so anyone reading can see exactly what's being built without hunting for the file.

06

System & Flow Diagram

How the pieces actually connect: the request flow, the decision points, where data moves. Usually the single thing that saves the most back-and-forth with engineering.

07

Technical Notes (optional)

For engineering context: dependencies, APIs, performance constraints. I only fill this in with input from engineering, never guesses.

08

Timeline

High-level phases or milestone dates. Rough is fine early on; the point is getting everyone aligned on the same shape of the schedule.

09

Stakeholders

Who's deciding, who's building, who needs to sign off, named, not just "the team," so nothing stalls waiting on an unclear owner.

10

GTM Plan (if applicable right away)

If this ships to users right away, how it goes out: who's told, what changes for them, and what we watch closely after launch.
